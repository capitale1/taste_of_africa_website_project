Jollof Rice

Jollof rice is the dish West Africa argues about most lovingly. Its roots are usually traced to the Wolof people of Senegal and the Gambia, whose rice and fish dish thieboudienne is often called jollof's ancestor. The name itself comes from the old Wolof (Jolof) Empire.

Today Nigerians, Ghanaians, Senegalese and Sierra Leoneans each cook it their own way, and the friendly "jollof wars" over whose version is best are part of the fun. No wedding, birthday or Christmas table is complete without it, and the slightly smoky, crispy layer at the bottom of the pot, known as party jollof flavor, is the most prized part.

  • ⏱ 1 hr 15 min
  • 🍽 Serves 4
  • 📍 Senegal, Nigeria, Ghana

Ingredients

  • 2 cups long-grain parboiled rice
  • 4 medium tomatoes
  • 2 red bell peppers
  • 1 scotch bonnet pepper
  • 2 onions
  • 3 tbsp tomato paste
  • 5 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 2 cups chicken or vegetable stock
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp grated ginger
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tsp salt (or to taste)

Instructions

Step 1: Make the pepper base
  1. Blend the tomatoes, red bell peppers, scotch bonnet and one onion until smooth.
  2. Pour into a pot and boil for about 15 minutes until it reduces by about a third.
Step 2: Fry the base
  1. Heat the oil in a large, heavy pot. Slice the second onion and fry until soft.
  2. Add the garlic, ginger and tomato paste. Fry for 3–4 minutes, stirring, until the paste darkens.
  3. Add the reduced pepper base, thyme, curry powder, bay leaves and salt. Cook for 10 minutes until the oil rises to the top.
Step 3: Cook the rice
  1. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear, then stir it into the sauce.
  2. Add the stock, cover the pot tightly (foil under the lid helps) and cook on low heat for 30 minutes.
  3. Stir gently once or twice. If the rice is still hard, add a splash of water and keep cooking.
Step 4: Get the smoky "party" flavor and serve
  1. When the rice is cooked, turn the heat up for 2–3 minutes without stirring so the bottom toasts slightly.
  2. Remove the bay leaves and serve with fried plantain, coleslaw or grilled chicken.
