Injera

Injera is the heart of every Ethiopian and Eritrean meal. This large, spongy flatbread is made from teff, a tiny grain native to the Ethiopian highlands, and fermented for several days, which gives it a pleasant sour taste and its famous bubbly surface.

Injera is plate, cutlery and food all at once. Stews such as doro wot and misir wot are spooned on top, and diners tear off pieces with their right hand to scoop them up. Sharing from one platter, and feeding a loved one a bite by hand (called gursha), is a sign of friendship and respect.

  • ⏱ 3 days (fermenting) + 30 min
  • 🍽 Makes about 8 injera
  • 📍 Ethiopia, Eritrea

Ingredients

  • 2 cups teff flour
  • 3 cups lukewarm water (plus extra for thinning)
  • 0.5 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp vegetable oil, for the pan

Instructions

Step 1: Mix the batter
  1. In a large glass or ceramic bowl, whisk the teff flour with 3 cups of lukewarm water until smooth and lump-free.
  2. Cover loosely with a clean cloth.
Step 2: Ferment for 2–3 days
  1. Leave the bowl at room temperature. After a day or two you will see bubbles and smell a sour aroma: that is the fermentation working.
  2. A layer of water may form on top. Pour most of it off before cooking.
Step 3: Prepare to cook
  1. Stir in the salt and enough water to make a thin batter, like pancake batter.
  2. Heat a large non-stick pan over medium heat and wipe it lightly with oil.
Step 4: Cook the injera
  1. Pour batter in a spiral from the outside in, then tilt the pan to cover the base.
  2. When holes appear across the surface, cover with a lid for 1–2 minutes until the top is set. Do not flip it.
  3. Slide onto a cloth to cool, and repeat with the rest of the batter.
