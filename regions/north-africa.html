North Africa

Warm spices, slow cooking and centuries of trade along the Mediterranean and the Sahara.

Food culture

North Africa includes Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya and Egypt. Its cuisine mixes the traditions of the Amazigh (Berber) people with Arab, Ottoman, Andalusian and French influences brought by trade and history.

Hospitality is central: guests are welcomed with sweet mint tea, and Friday couscous is a family tradition in many homes. Dishes are fragrant rather than fiery, seasoned with cumin, saffron, cinnamon, ginger and spice blends such as ras el hanout, with spicy harissa paste on the side.

Staple foods

  • Couscous
  • Wheat bread
  • Chickpeas
  • Lentils
  • Olives
  • Dates
  • Preserved lemons
  • Lamb

Cooking techniques

  • Meat and vegetables are slow-cooked in a cone-lidded clay tagine, which recycles steam to keep food tender.
  • Couscous is steamed two or three times over a simmering stew in a pot called a couscoussier.
  • Lemons are preserved in salt for weeks, giving a soft, salty-sour flavor to many dishes.
