West Africa

Bold, spicy and generous: West African cooking is built for sharing.

Food culture

West Africa stretches from Senegal to Nigeria and includes countries like Ghana, Mali, Côte d'Ivoire and Sierra Leone. Meals are social events, often eaten from a shared bowl, and food plays a central role in weddings, naming ceremonies and religious holidays.

Most dishes pair a starchy staple with a rich soup or stew. Flavor comes from scotch bonnet peppers, tomatoes, onions and red palm oil, deepened with fermented ingredients like dawadawa (locust beans) and dried or smoked fish.

Staple foods

  • Rice
  • Yams
  • Cassava
  • Plantain
  • Millet
  • Maize
  • Black-eyed peas
  • Groundnuts

Cooking techniques

  • Many soups are thickened with ground egusi (melon seeds), okra or groundnut paste.
  • Starchy foods such as yam and cassava are boiled and pounded into smooth, stretchy fufu, eaten by hand with soup.
  • Street food favorite suya is thinly sliced meat coated in a peanut and chili spice mix, then grilled over open flame.
