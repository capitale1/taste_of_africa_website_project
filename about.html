About Us

Taste of Africa is a celebration of the food, people and stories of the African continent.

Our mission

We want to promote and protect Africa's food heritage by sharing its recipes, ingredients and the stories behind them. Too often, African food is seen as one single cuisine. In reality, it is hundreds of traditions shaped by different landscapes, peoples and histories.

Whether you grew up eating these dishes or are discovering them for the first time, we hope this site helps you cook them, understand them and share them with the people you love.

What we do

Share recipes

Clear, step-by-step recipes for classic dishes from across the continent, with videos to cook along with.

Tell the stories

Every dish has a history. We explain where it comes from, who cooks it and when it is eaten.

Celebrate ingredients

From teff and fonio to cassava leaves, we highlight the crops and spices that make African cooking unique.

A short history of African cuisine

Ancient roots

Africa is one of the world's oldest centers of farming. Crops such as sorghum, pearl millet, African rice, teff, yams, okra and watermelon were first grown on the continent, and coffee comes from the highlands of Ethiopia. Many of these foods are still everyday staples today.

Trade and exchange

For centuries, trade routes connected Africa with the wider world. Camel caravans crossed the Sahara carrying salt, gold and spices, and ships on the Indian Ocean linked the Swahili coast with Arabia, Persia and India. This is why you find cardamom, cloves and pilau rice in East Africa, and saffron and cumin in North Africa.

From the 1500s, ships also brought crops from the Americas, including cassava, maize, peanuts, tomatoes and chili peppers. African cooks made them their own so completely that dishes like jollof rice, isombe and groundnut stew are now impossible to imagine without them.

Global influence

African food has also shaped kitchens around the world. Enslaved Africans carried their knowledge of rice farming, okra, black-eyed peas and one-pot cooking to the Americas, where they influenced dishes such as gumbo, rice and peas and many Caribbean and Brazilian classics. Today, African chefs and restaurants are introducing a new generation to these flavors across Europe, North America and beyond.

Visit a food landmark

Kimironko Market in Kigali, Rwanda, is one of the city's busiest markets. Its stalls are full of fresh produce, beans, plantains, cassava leaves for isombe, spices and colorful fabrics, making it a great place to see everyday East African food culture up close.
